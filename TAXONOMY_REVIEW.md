# Category Taxonomy Review — Step 2

**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Scope:** Validate the proposed 16-category / two-level structure against
professional UK event-industry sources. Produce a revised taxonomy with
rationale, plus a first-pass tag list per category.

**Lens:** Ballpark serves professional event agencies (brand activations,
exhibition stands, product launches, corporate experiences — clients like
Nike, TikTok, HelloFresh, Lavazza). Sources prioritised at that level:
exhibition contractors (GES, Quadrant2Design), production AV (White Light,
PRG), trade-furniture hire (Europa International), the trade body (ESSA),
exhibition freight forwarders, and corporate caterers (Moving Venue,
Social Pantry). Consumer marketplaces (Add to Event) used only as one
data point for category *structure*, not naming.

**Model recap (from Liam):**
- **Category** — one per item.
- **Subcategory** — one per item. The *type* of service/product.
- **Tags** — many per item. The *attributes* that describe it.
- A reference site's *service types* → our subcategories.
- A reference site's *filters* (cuisine, dietary, style, size) → our tags.

---

## Cross-cutting findings (read these first)

These five issues recur across categories and matter more than any single
subcategory tweak.

### 1. Missing "Equipment" home — the biggest structural gap
The Catering brief says *"catering equipment → Equipment or Supplies"* — but
neither **Equipment** nor **Supplies** exists in the 16 categories. Catering
equipment (ovens, fridges, urns, hot cupboards), glassware, crockery, and
AV-equipment-only dry hire have nowhere clean to live. Event Hire UK and
Expo Hire treat catering equipment as a top-level category.
**Recommendation:** either (a) add a 17th category **Equipment & Hire**, or
(b) decide explicitly that equipment always rides inside its discipline
category (catering equipment → Catering, AV kit → AV). Option (b) is
simpler and fits the two-level model — but it must be a stated rule, not
left ambiguous.

### 2. Type vs. attribute leakage — the dominant theme
Many proposed "subcategories" are actually *attributes* and belong in tags:
cuisine (Indian, Italian…), material (dried/artificial), branded/
colour-matched, turnaround (same-day edit), floor type (carpet/raised),
drink type (wine/cocktail/soft), meal occasion (breakfast/lunch),
staff seniority. The revised taxonomy below pushes ~25 of these down to
tags. This is the single biggest simplification and it is exactly the
type/attribute split Liam defined.

### 3. Insurance is duplicated
"Public Liability Insurance" sits under **Health & Safety** *and*
"Event Insurance" sits under **Logistics & Transport**. Insurance is one
thing — pick one home. Recommendation: keep it under Health & Safety
(it's a compliance/risk product), drop it from Logistics.

### 4. Scent is duplicated
"Fragrance & Scent" under **Florals** and "Scent / Aroma Design" under
**Event Accessories**. Recommendation: keep it in Event Accessories
(scent design is sensory-branding, not floristry), drop from Florals.

### 5. Construction is the weakest category
It merges build *trades* (joinery, paint, install labour) with build
*deliverables* (window display, immersive environment, photo moment).
A trade and a deliverable are different things. Detail in the Construction
section — it needs the most decisions from you.

**Net result:** 16 categories stay (no merges/splits at category level —
they're sound). The work is at subcategory level: **~135 proposed
subcategories → ~95 revised**, with the difference re-homed as tags.

**Open question for you:** Construction and Stand Structure split
"the structure" from "the labour to build it" — but most contractors sell
*design-and-build as one service*. The split is defensible (a freelance

be crisp: **Stand Structure = the finished thing you hire; Construction =
trades/crew you hire by skill or day.** If that line blurs in practice,
consider merging the two.

**First-pass tags:** trade-skill · finish-type (paint · spray · laminate ·
veneer) · material (timber · metal · composite) · crew-size

# Step 3 — Tag Dimensions (proposed)

**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Scope:** Proper tag dimensions for all 15 supplier-facing categories.
"Other" is excluded — it's internal agency line-items (PM fee,
contingency, travel), no supplier attributes.

**Model:** Tags are name–value pairs. The **dimension** is the name
(e.g. `cuisine`); the **value** is what the supplier picks when listing
(e.g. `Italian`). 4–6 dimensions per category, 5–15 values per dimension.
Dimensions derived from the FILTER panels of the Step 1/2 reference
sites. Values are what a *supplier self-selects* — professional event
production level, not consumer.

**Shared dimensions** (deliberate — same name → consistent filter UX):
`setting` appears in 9 categories · `branded` in 3 · `crewed` / `staffed`
in 4 · **`event-type` in 13** (see below).

Totals: 15 categories · ~76 dimensions · ~430 values.

---

## ⭐ `event-type` — the cross-cutting dimension (added after review)

The existing `items.tags TEXT[]` column is **already a de-facto
event-type dimension** — every one of its 55 distinct values is an event
type (`Gala`, `Product Launch`, `Activation`, `Conference`, `Awards`…).
It was never a controlled vocabulary, so it's full of duplicates
(`Activation`/`Brand Activation`, `Corporate Event`/`Corporate Events`,
`Press`/`Press Event`/`Press Events`, `Exhibition`/`Trade Show`).

`event-type` is therefore a **shared dimension** — like `setting` — that
applies to **13 of the 15 categories** (everything except Health & Safety
and Logistics & Transport, where "what event it suits" is meaningless).
It answers: *which kinds of event is this supplier's product good for.*

```
event-type:  Brand Activation, Product Launch, Exhibition / Trade Show,
             Conference, AGM / Panel, Gala, Awards Ceremony,
             Corporate Party, Corporate Dining, Drinks Reception,
             Press / Influencer Event, Pop-Up / Retail, Festival,
             Premiere / Screening, Wedding, Summer Party
```

**Migration note:** the 55 existing free-text `items.tags` values map
onto these 16 controlled values — *except* `Outdoor` / `Outdoor Event`,
which are not event types and migrate to `setting = Outdoor` instead.
A one-time cleanup script handles this when the tag schema is seeded.

Each category block below lists its own dimensions; assume `event-type`
is also present on all 13 applicable categories (omitted from each block
to avoid repetition).

## Step 3 — notes for review

- **Capacity bands** (Stand Structure m², AV pax, Venue pax) use the
  round numbers suppliers actually quote in — "up to 500 pax" is verbatim
  from the existing item seed data.
- **`cuisine` / `genre` carry an N/A value** so an equipment-only or
  non-musical supplier isn't forced to mis-tag.
- **Some dimensions are near-single-value in practice** (`branded` on
  Graphics is almost always "Yes") — kept because the filter still helps
  the browsing agency.
- **tier overlap (open question):** `service-level: Standard/Premium/Luxury`
  on Catering and `finish: Standard/Premium/Luxury` on Event Accessories
  duplicate the existing `items.tier` field (basic / mid / premium).
  Recommendation — keep `tier` as its own field, drop those two dimensions
  from the tag set. Awaiting your call before seeding.

Nothing seeded — review the dimensions/values, mark changes, then the
seed prompt follows.
